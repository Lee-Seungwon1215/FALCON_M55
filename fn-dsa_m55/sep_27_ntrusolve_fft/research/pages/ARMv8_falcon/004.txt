F , G. ffSampling process is a randomized rounding process
that generates s1 and s2. The main idea of ffSampling process
is to calculate the Closest Vector Problem (CVP) in a lower
dimension and lift it to a higher dimension. In ffSampling
process, vector representation in low dimension is simi-
lar to bit-reversed representation of FFT; therefore, it can
be easily calculated using the Butterfly Method based on
Cooley-Tukey and Gentleman-Sande algorithm [7]–[9]. This
mechanism is also applied in LDL∗operations called from
ffSampling process; thus, there are different versions of FFT
in Falcon, including splitfft. Finally, Sign process outputs the
random seed r and the compressed value s of s2 as a signature.
Verify process of Falcon, as shown in Algorithm 3, has
a quite simple structure compared to other PQC-DSAs
such as Crystals-Dilithum and Rainbow. In Verify process,
s1ands2 are recalculated and checked if ||(s1,s2)||2 ≤⌊β2⌋
is satisfied. If that bound ⌊β2⌋holds for the s1 and s2, then
the signature (r,s) is accepted.
B. FFT/NTT-BASED POLYNOMIAL MULTIPLICATION
Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) is an algorithm proposed to
fast compute discrete convolutions. FFT-based multiplication
is known as the fastest polynomial multiplication algorithm
which processes polynomial multiplication with only O(n·
logn) complexity via the Divide and conquer algorithm. FFT
divides the polynomial into its smallest unit by n-th of unity
and uses the Divide and conquer algorithm to perform point-
by-point multiplication at complexity O(n). Since FFT-based
multiplication in Falcon performs multiplication on the ele-
ments of a polynomial ring of rational coefficients, unlike
NTT, Montgomery-reduction is not required.
Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) is a variant of FFT
and is the polynomial multiplication algorithm used by many
lattice-based PKE/KEM and DSA schemes that have entered
the NIST PQC-competition Round 3 [10]. In general poly-
nomial rings using Zq[x]/ < 2n +1 > the condition for
using NTT is that n must be a power of 2 and q must be
congruent with 1 modulo 2n. Based on the NTT, the formula
for polynomial multiplication is:
f (x) ×g(x)=NTT−1(NTT (f ) ⊙NTT (g)), where ⊙is the
point-wise multiplication of the coefficients.
