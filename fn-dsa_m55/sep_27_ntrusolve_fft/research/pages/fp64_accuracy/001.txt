1 Introduction
Falcon [Pre+20] is one of the recently selected digital signatures for standard-
ization by the National Institute of Standards and Technology. Essentially the
signature is sampled with a probability approximated by floating-point numbers.
Since floating-point arithmetic is not always constant-time, [Por19] implemented
a series of constant-time floating-point arithmetic with software emulation. We
show that
– theemulatedfloating-pointmultiplicationdoesnothonoritsbehaviorclaimed
by [Por19];
– the discrepancy does not affect the complex fast Fourier transform in the
signature generation of Falcon; and
– how to prove the equivalence between emulated floating-point addition/sub-
traction/multiplication implementations.
