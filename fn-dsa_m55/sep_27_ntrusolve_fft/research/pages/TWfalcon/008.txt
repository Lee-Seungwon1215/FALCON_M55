3 Falcon Precision Proof
Falcon relies on Gaussian sampling for its signature generation. In practice, sampling
from an exact Gaussian distribution Dis not feasible due to the reliance on floating-point
arithmetic. As discussed in Section 2.2, floating-point numbers have limited precision,
which introduces rounding errors. Consequently, the practical distribution Dδ will be
different from the exact distribution D, where Dδ is close to Dparameterized by δ.
The extent of the difference depends on the precision of the floating-point arithmetic
used. [Pre17] shows a method to use the Rényi divergence to get a tighter bound (compared
to previous work) for the difference between the real and estimated distributions for Klein’s
sampler. This results in an estimated precision of 61 bits for λ= 256. Falcon uses a fast
Fourier variant of Klein’s sampler, thus they use a similar approach to the analysis of
Klein’s sampler done in [Pre17]. In this section the idea behind the analysis in Falcon is
explained. Then, a closer look at the precision analysis results is taken to figure out what
the result of the precision experiments done in Falcon mean for security. These experiments
are recreated on the latest Falcon reference code, and show a different result than stated
in the Falcon specification [Por25a, PFH+22]. From these experiments, a conclusion is
drawn for how many precision bits the Gaussian sampler is provably secure. Lastly, to
get enough precision to abide by the proven upper bound, the use of TW is given as an
alternative to the IEEE 754 double-precision floating-point values, which is investigated
more in Section 4.
